# Overhead Smash

## 1. Lesson Recap 🧠

The overhead smash is your put-away shot — the downward, aggressive strike you use when an opponent floats a ball high enough for you to reach above your head. When a lob or a high, soft ball comes your way, the smash is how you turn that gift into a point.

The goal of the smash is not raw violence. It is controlled, well-placed power. A smash that lands in the court at 70% effort wins far more points than a wild 100% swing that sails long. Think of it less like swinging an axe and more like throwing a ball hard at a target on the ground.

When you hit it on balance and aim it into open court, the overhead smash is one of the most reliable point-enders in the game.

## 2. Big Idea 💡

Throw the paddle at the ball — high point, slightly in front, controlled power.

If you remember one thing, make it the throwing motion. The smash is the same action as throwing a ball overhand: load the shoulder, reach up, and snap through contact at the highest comfortable point a little in front of your body. Get under the ball early and let the throw do the work, instead of muscling it with a stiff arm.

## 3. Key Cues 🎯

- "Turn sideways and load."
- "Point at the ball with your off hand."
- "Get under it — small steps, not a lunge."
- "Reach up and out — contact high and in front."
- "Throw the paddle, don't push it."
- "Snap the wrist, finish down."
- "Aim, don't just hit hard."
- "Hit at the feet or into the open angle."
- "Recover to the line."

## 4. Step-by-Step 👣

1. Read it early. The instant the ball goes up, turn your shoulders sideways to the net and start moving.
2. Track the ball with your non-paddle hand by pointing up at it. This keeps your shoulders turned and helps you judge the contact point.
3. Use small shuffle or crossover steps to get your body under and slightly behind the ball, so contact will be in front of you — not behind your head.
4. Load the paddle up behind your head, like you are about to throw a ball, with your elbow up and weight on your back foot.
5. Reach up and make contact at the highest comfortable point, slightly in front of your body, with your arm nearly fully extended.
6. Throw through the ball using shoulder rotation and a wrist snap, transferring your weight from back foot to front foot.
7. Aim with purpose — hit down at your opponent's feet, into the open court, or at the gap between players. Power without a target is wasted.
8. Recover. Bring the paddle back up in front of you and move back to the kitchen line, ready for the next ball.

## 5. Common Mistakes ⚠️

- Letting the ball get behind you: Contact behind your head produces a weak, off-balance shot. Move your feet early so the ball stays in front of you.
- Swinging too hard: Maximum effort usually means the ball sails long or you lose control. Dial it back to controlled power and you will make far more of them.
- Standing flat-footed: Reaching straight up without setting your feet leaves you stretched and unbalanced. Take small steps to get under the ball first.
- Pushing instead of throwing: A stiff, arm-only "push" has no snap or power. Use the full throwing motion with shoulder and wrist.
- No target: Smashing the ball straight back at a ready opponent invites a counter. Aim at feet or open space instead.
- Watching your shot instead of recovering: Admiring the smash leaves you out of position. Reset to the line in case it comes back.

## 6. Self-Check ✅

- Did I turn sideways and point at the ball with my off hand?
- Did I move my feet to get under the ball, with contact in front of me?
- Was my contact point high, near full extension?
- Did I throw through the ball instead of pushing it?
- Did I aim at a target — feet or open court — instead of just swinging hard?
- Did the ball travel down into the court rather than floating long?
- Did I recover to the kitchen line after the shot?

## 7. Homework 🏠

Practice these before your next play session:

- Throwing motion drill: With no paddle, practice throwing a ball overhand against a wall or to a partner for 2 minutes. This grooves the same shoulder-and-wrist action the smash uses.
- Shadow smash: Without a ball, rehearse the full sequence — turn sideways, point with the off hand, shuffle under, reach up, throw, and recover. Do 15 reps focusing on smooth footwork.
- Self-toss smash: Toss a ball up for yourself and hit 20 controlled overheads into the court. Aim for clean contact at a high point, not maximum power.
- Partner feed drill: Have a partner feed easy, high balls you can reach. Hit 20 smashes, aiming half at a target near their feet and half into open court. Track how many land in.
- Footwork-under-the-ball drill: Have a partner toss balls slightly behind you so you must shuffle back to set up. Do 10 reps to build the habit of getting under the ball instead of reaching.
- Awareness task: During recreational play, notice which balls are high enough to smash and commit to taking them out of the air instead of letting them bounce.

## 8. Game Application 🎮

Use the overhead smash whenever an opponent gives you a ball high enough to comfortably reach above your head — most often a short lob or a popped-up dink. The smash is your reward for patient kitchen play, so take it when it is offered.

Be selective. If the ball is too deep or behind you, do not force a hero smash from the back of the court — a controlled overhead or a reset keeps you in the point. The smash is for balls you can attack on balance, not for every high ball.

In doubles, placement beats power. Aim at the feet of the player closest to you, or hit into the open gap between opponents. A well-placed 70% smash that they cannot return cleanly is far more effective than a 100% swing that misses or pops up for a counter. After you smash, get your paddle up and reset to the line — good players will block it back.

## 9. Visualization 👁️

Before you play, picture yourself at the kitchen line, relaxed and ready. An opponent floats a lob up, and you recognize it instantly.

See yourself turn sideways, point up at the ball with your off hand, and take quick, light steps to settle under it. You feel balanced, weight loaded on your back foot, paddle cocked behind your head. As the ball drops into your hitting zone, you reach up and throw the paddle through it at full extension, snapping crisply at contact. You hear the clean pop, watch the ball drive down into the open court at your opponent's feet, and smoothly bring your paddle back up as you recover to the line — calm, controlled, and in command of the point.

## 10. Quick Reminders 🎗️

- Turn sideways and point at the ball.
- Move your feet — contact high and in front.
- Throw the paddle; don't push it.
- Controlled power beats max effort.
- Aim at the feet or the open gap.
- Skip the hero smash on deep balls — reset instead.
- Recover to the line; expect it back.
