# Student Handout Boilerplate Template

Use this template to create a student-facing handout for a specific pickleball technique, strategy, or lesson topic.

Keep the language clear, practical, encouraging, and appropriate for beginner to intermediate adult students. Avoid overly technical explanations unless they are necessary, and when technical terms are used, explain them simply.

## Output Format

- Use the exact section headings and emojis shown in this template.
- Include the lesson topic at the top of the handout.
- Include the student's name and lesson date only if they are provided by the user.
- Keep the final handout easy to scan, with short paragraphs and concise bullet points where helpful.
- Keep the tone encouraging, practical, and coach-like.
- Do not include the template instructions in the final student handout.
- Avoid repeating the same content in multiple sections. Each section should have a distinct purpose.
- Do not frame the handout around a lesson or a point in time. Write it as a straightforward reference about the skill itself, so it reads correctly no matter when the student reviews it. Avoid lesson- or time-anchored phrasing such as "today," "today's lesson," "in our last lesson," "this lesson covers," or "this lesson covered." Instead of "This lesson covers the dink volley...," open by describing the skill directly, for example "The dink volley is...".

Each section heading must use the exact same emoji shown in this template. The emoji is part of the section title and should remain unchanged when creating student handouts from this template.

Do not replace, remove, or substitute the emojis unless the user specifically asks for a change.

## PDF and Layout Guidance

When formatting the handout as a PDF:

- A numbered section must never split across pages. The whole section (heading plus its content) stays on one page.
- The build script enforces this automatically by keeping each section together, so a section that does not fit in the remaining space moves to the next page as a whole. This can leave white space at the bottom of a page, which is acceptable.
- If a single section is too tall to fit on one page on its own, it will be forced to break. When that happens, shorten the section (fewer or tighter bullets) so it fits on one page.
- If needed, compact spacing or font size slightly in the Resources section, but keep it readable.
- Always visually check the final rendered PDF (the preview PNGs) before delivering it. Do not rely only on text extraction or page count; inspect the rendered pages and confirm no section is split.

## Section Organization Rules

- Practice drills and assignments belong in `7. Homework 🏠`.
- Quick reminders and pre-play review cues belong in `10. Quick Reminders 🎗️`.
- Outside links and reference materials belong in `11. Resources 🔗`.
- Do not place duplicate practice drills or repeated review lists in Resources.
- Resources should support the lesson, not repeat the entire handout.

## 1. Lesson Recap 🧠

Provide a brief overview of the skill or strategy. This section should remind the student of the main topic, the purpose of the skill or strategy, and why it matters during actual play.

Keep this section short and readable. It should function as a memory jogger, not a full transcript. Include the most important takeaways in plain language.

Do not frame this section around a lesson or a point in time. Open by describing the skill or strategy directly, for example "The dink volley is...", rather than "This lesson covers the dink volley..." or "Today's lesson focused on...", since the student may read the handout at any time.

## 2. Big Idea 💡

Identify the central concept the student should remember. This should be the one main principle that ties the entire lesson together.

The Big Idea should be simple enough for a student to recall during play. It should answer the question: "What is the main thing I need to understand about this skill or strategy?"

Avoid listing multiple unrelated ideas here. Keep it focused.

## 3. Key Cues 🎯

List the short phrases, reminders, or coaching cues that help the student perform the skill correctly. These should be concise and easy to remember during play.

Use cues that are actionable and specific. Avoid vague phrases unless they are explained. For example, instead of only saying "stay balanced," explain what that means physically, such as keeping the feet under the body and avoiding lunging.

This section may include mental cues, body-position cues, timing cues, target cues, and recovery cues.

## 4. Step-by-Step 👣

Break the technique or strategy into clear, sequential steps. The student should be able to read this section and understand what to do first, second, third, and so on.

Each step should describe a specific action or decision. Keep the steps practical and observable. Avoid making the sequence too long; include only the steps that matter most for correct execution.

For techniques, include body position, paddle position, footwork, contact point, swing path, target, and recovery when relevant.

For strategies, include recognition, decision-making, communication, shot choice, court movement, and recovery when relevant.

## 5. Common Mistakes ⚠️

Describe the most common errors students make when learning this skill or strategy. For each mistake, explain why it causes a problem and, when helpful, include a simple correction.

Do not make this section sound discouraging. Frame mistakes as normal learning points. The goal is to help students recognize and correct problems during practice or play.

Focus on mistakes that are likely for beginner to intermediate students.

## 6. Self-Check ✅

Provide a short list of questions the student can ask themselves while practicing or playing. These questions should help the student evaluate whether they are applying the lesson correctly.

Self-check questions should be simple, specific, and tied directly to the lesson. They should help the student notice body position, timing, shot choice, target, balance, communication, or recovery.

Use questions that begin with phrases like:

- Am I...?
- Did I...?
- Was I...?
- Did the ball...?
- Did I recover...?

## 7. Homework 🏠

Give the student specific practice assignments to do before the next lesson, clinic, or practice session. This section should focus on repetition and skill development.

Homework is the main place for practice drills. Include solo drills, partner drills, wall drills, shadow swings, footwork practice, video review, or awareness tasks during recreational play when they fit the lesson.

Make the homework realistic and manageable. Avoid assigning too many drills. Include clear instructions, suggested repetitions or time limits, and the purpose of each activity.

Do not repeat these drills later in Resources.

## 8. Game Application 🎮

Explain how the student should use the skill or strategy during real games. This section should bridge the gap between controlled practice and live play.

Include guidance on when to use the skill, when not to force it, what result to aim for, and how to stay patient while learning.

Emphasize practical decision-making over perfection. Help the student understand how the lesson fits into doubles play, point construction, positioning, communication, or shot selection when applicable.

## 9. Visualization 👁️

Provide a short visualization exercise the student can use before playing or while mentally rehearsing away from the court.

The visualization should guide the student through imagining the skill or strategy being performed correctly. Include sensory details when useful, such as seeing the ball, feeling balance, hearing the paddle contact, and watching the ball travel to the intended target.

Keep the script simple and specific. Focus on calm execution, correct movement, smart decision-making, and confidence. Avoid making the visualization overly dramatic or unrealistic.

## 10. Quick Reminders 🎗️

Provide a brief pre-play checklist for the student to review before a game, open play, practice session, or clinic. This section should be shorter than Homework.

The purpose is to refresh the student's memory immediately before play. Include only the most important reminders they should carry onto the court.

This section should focus on application, not training volume. It should answer: "What should I remember right before I try this in a real point?"

Do not include full practice drills here. Keep this section to quick cues, priorities, and decision reminders.

## 11. Resources 🔗

This section must always appear at the very end of the handout.

Resources should provide optional outside review away from the court. They should not repeat the Homework section or duplicate the Quick Reminders checklist.

Use current web research when generating this section. Resource links, article availability, YouTube titles, channels, and view counts may change over time, so verify them at the time the handout is created.

Keep this section organized, relevant, and not overwhelming.

The Resources section must include the categories below.

### Articles

Include exactly three articles with links.

The three articles must come from three different websites or sources. Do not include two articles from the same website.

Articles should come from well-regarded sources, such as:

- USA Pickleball
- Established pickleball coaching organizations
- Reputable instructional websites
- Major pickleball brands with strong educational content

Each article should include:

- Title
- Source
- Link
- One short description explaining why it is useful for the student

### YouTube Videos

Include exactly three YouTube videos with links.

The three videos must come from three different YouTube creators or channels. Do not include two videos from the same creator or channel.

Select the top three most-viewed relevant videos available for the lesson topic, while still following the source-variety rule. If the highest-viewed videos repeat the same creator, choose the next most-viewed relevant video from a different creator.

The videos must directly relate to the skill, strategy, or lesson being taught and should match the beginner to intermediate level whenever possible.

Each video should include:

- Title
- Channel name
- Link
- One short description explaining why it is useful for the student

Because YouTube view counts can change, select the most-viewed relevant videos based on the best available current information at the time the handout is created.

### Rules and Strategy References

Include only rule, positioning, or strategy references that help the student understand when and why the skill is used.

Use this subsection for outside references, such as official rules, court-positioning explainers, or credible strategy articles. Do not use it for repeating drills, homework assignments, or the main review checklist.

Each reference should include:

- Title
- Source
- Link
- One short description explaining why it supports the lesson
